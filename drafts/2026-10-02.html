    Home-Start London research finds one in four families with under-5s lack safe space at home for their child to play Research: housing crisis and early childhood development★★★

    Home-Start London has published 'Family Voices 2026: Holding it together at home', based on the experiences of 350 London families with children under five across its 14-borough network. The report finds 25% of families say their home does not provide enough safe space for their child to crawl, play or move, and calls on London politicians and councils to recognise housing insecurity as a crisis for childhood. Shelter and the 4in10 Child Poverty Network are among the organisations backing the call.

    Why it matters to Babyzone: This evidence directly strengthens the case for accessible, out-of-home early years spaces — Babyzone could explore whether this research supports funder or policy arguments for why its drop-in hub model is essential for families in overcrowded or insecure housing in London.

    Suggested action: Comms & Content lead to consider sharing or publicly responding to this research given its alignment with Babyzone's mission; Policy & Impact lead to note it as supporting evidence for hub value propositions in London-facing funder conversations.

    Government rolls out Healthy Babies programme and expanded health visiting to all 1,000 family hubs in England Family Hubs / Sure Start programme expansion★★★★

    The Health Secretary announced that the Healthy Babies programme will expand to every local authority area in England, embedding enhanced health visiting support across all 1,000 family hubs and reviving Sure Start-style integrated family support. The Local Government Association welcomed additional social care workforce support announced alongside the programme; the Institute of Health Visiting welcomed the commitment to women and babies but sector voices, including in the nursing press, warn the expansion cannot succeed without a sufficient health visitor workforce.

    Why it matters to Babyzone: This is the largest government investment in integrated early years family hub infrastructure in years, directly shaping the commissioning and funding landscape in which Babyzone operates; it reinforces the policy case for trusted, barrier-free hub support and may open new LA-level partnership or commissioning conversations for Babyzone's model.

    Suggested action: Policy & Impact lead to track LA-level implementation timelines and any associated funding streams flowing to local authorities; Fundraising lead to use this as narrative context in funder conversations; Leadership to consider whether Babyzone should make a public statement positioning its hub model as complementary to the statutory health visiting offer.

    Owner: Leadership · Urgency: this_week

    Watchlist: Sure Start / Family Hubs programme, MHCLG (Family Hubs), Institute of Health Visiting

    The Institute of Health Visiting has announced a new national initiative, developed with Professor Monica Lakhanpaul at University College London, to co-design culturally grounded and trauma-informed health visiting support for families living in temporary accommodation. The initiative focuses explicitly on the consequences of temporary housing for children's health, development, education and sense of security in their earliest years.

    Why it matters to Babyzone: Families in temporary accommodation are among the most disadvantaged and hardest-to-reach groups that Babyzone's hubs could serve; IHV's framing of trauma-informed, culturally grounded practice closely mirrors Babyzone's own approach to families reluctant to access statutory services, and this initiative could open a research co-design or delivery partnership route.

    Suggested action: Policy & Impact lead to monitor the initiative's progress and explore whether Babyzone hubs could contribute as a community delivery context or co-design partner, particularly in areas with high temporary accommodation populations.
