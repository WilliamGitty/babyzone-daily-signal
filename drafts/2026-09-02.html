Research & Insights

Mental health A&E attendances among children aged 6–9 have risen 62%, new data shows ★★★ medium

New analysis reported by CYP Now shows that mental health-related A&E attendances among children aged six to nine have risen by 62%. The data indicates that a significant mental health crisis is emerging well before adolescence, with young primary-school-age children presenting in acute distress at emergency departments at sharply increasing rates.

What this means for Babyzone: What this means for Babyzone — Although 6–9 year olds are older than Babyzone's primary 0–5 cohort, this data makes a powerful case for earlier intervention. Children presenting in mental health crisis at primary school age are very likely to have experienced adversity, insecure attachment, or unmet developmental need long before school entry. This strengthens the evidence base for Babyzone's model — supporting parental confidence, emotional wellbeing, and child development from pregnancy onwards — and is worth citing in funding applications and impact narratives as a downstream cost-of-inaction argument.
