UN discussions advance towards recognising early childhood education as a universal right ★★ low

Discussions at the United Nations are progressing towards a possible international agreement that would formally recognise early childhood education as a fundamental right. The process remains at a formative stage and no binding agreement has been reached.

What this means for Babyzone: What this means for Babyzone: a global normative shift towards early childhood education as a right rather than a discretionary benefit would strengthen the international evidence and advocacy environment for UK organisations arguing for sustained public investment in the 0–5 period. While the timeline for any binding agreement is uncertain, the direction of travel supports the case Babyzone makes to funders and policymakers about the foundational, non-optional nature of early years support.

Scotland pledges to extend funded childcare to operate year-round ★★ medium

The Scottish Government has committed to expanding its funded childcare offer so that it runs year-round, removing provision gaps during school holidays. The announcement extends the existing funded hours entitlement rather than introducing new eligibility categories.

What this means for Babyzone: What this means for Babyzone: Scotland's year-round commitment illustrates how policy can close the holiday cliff-edge in early years provision — gaps that disproportionately affect families in disadvantaged areas. Babyzone's hubs already offer a consistent, non-term-time presence, and this policy direction reinforces the case for flexible, community-based provision that does not disappear during holidays when families may be most isolated — a point worth highlighting in funding applications and policy briefings to English commissioners.
